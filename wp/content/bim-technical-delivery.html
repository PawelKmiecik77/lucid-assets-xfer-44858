Technical Delivery, Coordinated in One BIM Model

Lucid uses BIM/Revit-led workflows to connect concept, coordination, documentation and implementation logic. The goal is not to model for presentation only, but to reduce risk, improve clarity and support delivery across multiple disciplines.

— Capability

BIM as the connective tissue across four disciplines.

How BIM connects Lighting, Signage, Urban Furniture and Art & Digital into one coordinated environment.

— 01

BIM as coordination framework

BIM/Revit is treated as the coordination framework that connects creative decisions across disciplines — not a presentation layer. Lighting, signage, urban furniture and art & digital data live within one model environment so the design intent is preserved through documentation and site.

— 02

Revit-ready workflow

We work with native Revit families where possible and structured RFA libraries for signage and custom objects. Where partners use other modelling environments, STEP, IFC and DWG bridges are coordinated under a clear data exchange plan.

— 03

Clash reduction and coordination

Coordination focuses on the spatial layers we contribute — light cones, signage clearance zones, art structural envelopes, urban furniture footprints — and their interaction with architecture, MEP, structure and landscape.

— 04

Technical models and documentation

Documentation is structured for tender, production and installation: technical drawings, schedules, specifications, control intent and material logic, all derived from the same coordinated model environment.

— 05

STEP & RFA families for signage

Signage typologies are delivered as Revit-ready RFA families with STEP documentation, supporting BIM-based sign distribution and downstream production coordination.

— 06

Implementation-ready packages

Final packages are organised so that contractors, fabricators and installers receive consistent, decision-supporting information from a single coordinated source.

— Proprietary layer

Two proprietary tools feed structured data straight into this BIM chain: WayDrafter for wayfinding & signage carriers, and the Urban Furniture Simulator for evidence-based placement of urban elements — both resolving into the same Revit / RFA / STEP / IFC model rather than sitting beside it.

— 01What file formats does Lucid deliver for coordination and fabrication?+
Revit families (RFA), STEP (ISO 10303), IFC and DWG, plus a bill of quantities extracted from the model — structured for tender, production and installation.
— 02What is a federated model and clash detection?+
A federated model combines Lucid's discipline data with architecture, structure, MEP and landscape in one environment, where clash detection resolves spatial conflicts — light cones, signage clearances, furniture footprints — before they reach site.
— 03How do the proprietary tools fit the BIM workflow?+
WayDrafter and the Urban Furniture Simulator feed structured design data into the same federated Revit model, so the proprietary layer and the coordinated model stay in sync rather than living in separate files.
